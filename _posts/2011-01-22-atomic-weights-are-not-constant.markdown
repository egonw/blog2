In case you missed it, "standard atomic weights are not constants of nature"
(doi:[10.1351/PAC-REP-10-09-14](http://dx.doi.org/10.1351/PAC-REP-10-09-14))!
Wow, chemistry upside down. This is bigger than the new arsenic life they found!

Calm down, calm down. Nothing to see here, move on.

It was actually news a some weeks back, but a [tweet by @MatToddChem](https://twitter.com/#!/MatToddChem/status/28787041277837312)
and a question by [Antony on the Blue Obelisk eXchange](http://blueobelisk.shapado.com/questions/for-the-calculation-of-masses-in-open-babel-where-is-the-table-of-atomic-masses-sourced-from),
made me write up this post.

Facts: 1. atomic weights have never been constant; 2. isotopic weight are constants
of nature. The difference is simple, but the public was amazed last month and
reaffirmed that science is just another religion. (In fact, the Dutch political
wizard Wilders calls religion just a politic ideology, so, science is just politics;
Q.E.D. :).

Atomic weights are used to calculate the weight of samples, or, the other way
around, how many molecules we have in 1 mg of some organic sample. Now, at this
macroscopic level, and looking at carbons, we have actually to do with a mix
of, mostly I guess, <sup>12</sup>C and <sup>13</sup>C, more or less 99% and
1% each. Now, these percentage reflect a mixture. Mixture composition has never
been natural constants, so the claim by the authors of the paper is weird, to
say the least. In fact, it has been know for years that the isotope ratios vary
around the world. Hence, the molecular weight of compound X is not the same
here as somewhere else. That's all.

Now, the [Blue Obelisk](http://www.blueobelisk.org) has been making this information
aggregated by IUPAC available under a permissive license. The most recent release
still has a MIT license, but the next release will be even more permissive an
have the CC0 waiver.
