[Nico](http://scimantica.com/blog/) asked recently about the [availability of
chemical data as RDF](http://blueobelisk.shapado.com/questions/chemical-data-in-rdf-who-are-the-providers).
There is no so much really. Finding large amounts of Open Data in chemistry
is in general a problem. Things are slowly changing, however, though it is not
very apparent.

About 1.5 years ago I started a [FriedFeed room Open Chemical Data](http://friendfeed.com/openchemicaldata)
where RSS feeds of new chemical data are aggregated. It started with the [RSS
feed from the NMRShiftDB](http://chem-bla-ics.blogspot.com/2009/09/open-chemical-data-1-nmrshiftdb.html),
and later ChemPedia Substances was added (which is expected to go EOL). I was
informed, however, about [Dryad](http://datadryad.org/). It is a website that
allows deposition of data published in journals, *as CC0*. That latter is amazing!
And when they announced [a new twitter feed](http://blog.datadryad.org/2011/01/31/dryad-newsletter-january-2011/),
also noted they had a RSS feed of new data sets. [I asked about feeds specific
for chemistry](http://blog.datadryad.org/2011/01/31/dryad-newsletter-january-2011/#comment-371),
which [prompted Ryan](http://blog.datadryad.org/2011/01/31/dryad-newsletter-january-2011/#comment-375)
to set up [this Yahoo Pipe](http://pipes.yahoo.com/ryscher/dryadchemical).

So, Dryad is now part of the [Open Chemical Data](http://friendfeed.com/openchemicaldata)
room (second item):
